Naloxone (Narcan): What It Is, How to Use It & How It Saves Lives

Naloxone is a medication that can reverse an opioid overdose within minutes. Since 2023, it is available over the counter at most US pharmacies without a prescription. This guide tells you everything you need to know — including step-by-step instructions for using Narcan nasal spray.

📅 August 11, 2026 ✍️ Reviewed by Andrew Gaon, PharmD ⏱ 9 min read
⚡ Quick Reference — Overdose Emergency:

Call 911 → Administer Narcan → Recovery position → Stay until help arrives. SAMHSA Helpline: 1-800-662-4357. Good Samaritan laws protect you in most states when calling 911 for an overdose.

What is Naloxone?

Naloxone is an opioid antagonist — a medication that rapidly reverses opioid overdose by blocking opioid receptors in the brain. It was first approved by the FDA in 1971 and has since been credited with saving hundreds of thousands of lives worldwide. In 2023, the FDA approved Narcan 4mg nasal spray as an over-the-counter product — the first opioid overdose reversal medication available without a prescription in the United States.

Naloxone works for all opioids — prescription painkillers (oxycodone, hydrocodone, morphine, codeine, fentanyl), heroin, and synthetic opioids. It is included on the WHO List of Essential Medicines and is recommended by the CDC, SAMHSA, NIH, and every major medical organization.

Drug Profile: Naloxone (Narcan)

Generic Name: Naloxone HCl
Brand Names: Narcan, Kloxxado, Revive
Drug Class: Opioid Antagonist
FDA Approval: 1971 (injection); 2015 (nasal spray)
OTC Status: Yes — since March 2023
Onset of Action: 2–5 minutes
Duration: 30–90 minutes
Forms Available: Nasal spray, auto-injector, injection

How Naloxone Works

Opioids cause overdose by binding to receptors in the brainstem that control breathing — causing respiratory depression (slowed or stopped breathing). Naloxone works by competitively binding to these same receptors with greater affinity than opioids, rapidly displacing the opioid molecules and restoring normal breathing.

The effects of naloxone begin within 2–5 minutes of administration. Because most opioids last longer than naloxone (30–90 min for naloxone vs. 4–12+ hours for most opioids), re-sedation can occur — making it critical to call 911 even if the person initially responds to naloxone.

Step-by-Step: How to Use Narcan Nasal Spray

  1. Call 911 immediately — even before administering naloxone
  2. Recognize the signs: blue lips, unresponsive, stopped/shallow breathing, gurgling sounds, pinpoint pupils
  3. Lay the person on their back and tilt head back slightly to open airway
  4. Hold the Narcan nasal spray — thumb on bottom, two fingers on nozzle
  5. Insert the nozzle into one nostril until your fingers touch the bottom of their nose
  6. Press the plunger firmly — administer the full dose
  7. If no response in 2–3 minutes — administer a second dose in the other nostril
  8. Place in recovery position (on their side) to prevent choking
  9. Continue rescue breathing if trained and person is not breathing
  10. Stay with the person until emergency services arrive — re-sedation can occur

Where to Get Naloxone (Narcan) Without a Prescription

Since the 2023 FDA OTC approval, naloxone is widely available:

  • Major pharmacies: CVS, Walgreens, Rite Aid, Walmart, Costco — no prescription needed
  • Free through harm reduction organizations — SAMHSA's findtreatment.gov lists local programs
  • Some state health departments distribute free naloxone
  • Community health centers and emergency departments
  • Online pharmacy — with valid prescription in some states

Special Considerations for Fentanyl Overdoses

Fentanyl's extreme potency (50–100x morphine) means that standard single doses of naloxone may not be enough. Key points for fentanyl overdoses:

  • Administer 2 doses back-to-back if no response within 2 minutes
  • Higher-concentration naloxone (Kloxxado 8mg) may be more effective
  • Fentanyl overdose can recur even after naloxone — never leave the person alone
  • SAMHSA recommends anyone in high-fentanyl communities carry at least 2 doses

Frequently Asked Questions About Naloxone

Do I need a prescription for naloxone (Narcan)?

No — since 2023, Narcan 4mg nasal spray is FDA-approved over the counter. Anyone can buy it at CVS, Walgreens, Rite Aid, Walmart and most other pharmacies without a prescription. Many harm reduction programs also provide it free of charge.

Is it safe to give naloxone if you're not sure opioids are involved?

Yes — naloxone is safe to give even if you're not certain opioids caused the overdose. If the person has not taken opioids, naloxone has absolutely no effect on them. It is completely safe to administer when in doubt. Always call 911 regardless.

How many doses are needed for a fentanyl overdose?

Due to fentanyl's extreme potency, 2–4 doses are often required. Administer a second dose if no response within 2–3 minutes. Keep administering every 2–3 minutes while awaiting emergency services. SAMHSA recommends carrying at least 2 doses in high-fentanyl communities.
