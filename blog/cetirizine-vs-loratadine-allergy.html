Cetirizine vs Loratadine: Which Allergy Pill Works Better?

By Promedic Medical Team  |  August 7, 2026  |  8 min read

Seasonal allergies affect over 50 million Americans every year, and two OTC antihistamines dominate the market: cetirizine (Zyrtec) and loratadine (Claritin). Both are second-generation antihistamines that block histamine H1 receptors to relieve sneezing, runny nose, itchy eyes, and hives — but they differ in potency, speed, and how much they make you drowsy. Here's exactly how to choose between them.

Key Takeaway: Cetirizine works faster and stronger — better for severe allergy symptoms and hives. Loratadine is truly non-drowsy — better when you need to stay sharp at work or drive. Both are taken once daily and are highly effective for seasonal and perennial allergies.

What Is Cetirizine?

Cetirizine 10mg (Zyrtec) is a second-generation antihistamine that works within 1 hour and provides relief for up to 24 hours. It is slightly more potent than loratadine and has a higher affinity for H1 receptors, which explains why it is considered more effective for intense symptoms like urticaria (hives), severe hay fever, and allergic skin reactions.

According to a review in the Annals of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology, cetirizine consistently outperforms loratadine in head-to-head trials for wheal-and-flare reactions (hive suppression) and nasal symptom scores. However, it does cross the blood-brain barrier slightly more, causing mild sedation in a subset of users.

What Is Loratadine?

Loratadine 10mg (Claritin) is also a second-generation antihistamine, but it is specifically designed with a larger molecular structure that limits its entry into the brain — making it the truest "non-drowsy" option among popular antihistamines. It takes slightly longer to kick in (1–3 hours) and is generally considered slightly less potent than cetirizine for severe symptoms.

Loratadine is particularly popular because it has minimal cognitive effects — meaning it does not impair driving, work performance, or reaction times. The Mayo Clinic notes that loratadine is one of the most studied and well-tolerated antihistamines available.

Which Is Better for Specific Allergy Types?

Seasonal Allergies (Hay Fever)

Both antihistamines are approved and effective for seasonal allergic rhinitis — the classic hay fever with sneezing, runny nose, and itchy eyes. Cetirizine may provide slightly faster, more complete relief, especially in the first few hours. For daily use throughout allergy season, both are comparable with consistent dosing. If you know pollen counts will be high, cetirizine gives you a stronger initial block.

Hives and Skin Allergies

Cetirizine is the preferred choice for urticaria (hives), angioedema, and allergic skin rashes. Multiple clinical studies demonstrate cetirizine's superior ability to suppress the skin wheal-and-flare response. If your allergy primarily involves itching, redness, or raised welts, cetirizine 10mg is the recommended OTC option.

Dust Mite and Pet Allergies

For year-round (perennial) allergies triggered by pet dander, dust mites, or mold, daily antihistamine use is often recommended. Both cetirizine and loratadine work well for this purpose. Choose loratadine if cognitive performance at work matters, or cetirizine if your symptoms are more severe.

Allergic Conjunctivitis (Itchy Eyes)

Both antihistamines help with eye symptoms, but cetirizine again shows a slight edge in clinical studies for eye itching specifically. For severe eye allergies, your doctor may also recommend antihistamine eye drops alongside an oral antihistamine.

Children's Allergies

Both are approved for children aged 2 and older (with age-appropriate dosing). Loratadine is often preferred for school-age children during the day because it avoids potential drowsiness that could affect concentration. Cetirizine may be better at night or on weekends when drowsiness is less of a concern.

Choose Cetirizine if you:

  • Have severe allergy symptoms (intense sneezing, itching, hives)
  • Need faster relief within the first hour
  • Primarily suffer from hives or skin allergies
  • Don't mind mild drowsiness (or take it at night)

Choose Loratadine if you:

  • Need to drive, operate machinery, or stay alert at work
  • Have mild-to-moderate seasonal allergy symptoms
  • Are sensitive to drowsiness effects
  • Want the most well-tolerated daily antihistamine

What About Diphenhydramine (Benadryl)?

Diphenhydramine 25mg (Benadryl) is a first-generation antihistamine that is significantly more sedating than both cetirizine and loratadine. It works faster (within 30 minutes) but causes pronounced drowsiness, impairs driving, and wears off in 4–6 hours rather than 24. Diphenhydramine is best reserved for:

  • Acute allergic reactions requiring fast action
  • Short-term nighttime allergy or sleep support
  • Situations where a stronger sedating antihistamine is needed under medical guidance

For daily allergy management, cetirizine or loratadine are far superior to diphenhydramine in terms of safety, tolerability, and duration of action.

Dosage and How to Take Them

Cetirizine Dosage

  • Adults and children 6+: 10mg once daily
  • Children 2–5: 5mg once daily (liquid formulation)
  • Timing: Can be taken at any time; many prefer evening to avoid any sedation during the day
  • With or without food: Either is fine
  • Kidney impairment: Reduce to 5mg daily if CrCl <31 mL/min

Loratadine Dosage

  • Adults and children 6+: 10mg once daily
  • Children 2–5: 5mg once daily
  • Timing: Any time of day; does not cause daytime drowsiness
  • With or without food: Either is fine
  • Liver impairment: Use every other day for severe hepatic impairment

Side Effects Comparison

Cetirizine Side Effects

  • Drowsiness or fatigue (most notable side effect, in ~10–15% of users)
  • Dry mouth
  • Headache
  • Stomach upset (rare)

Loratadine Side Effects

  • Headache (most common)
  • Dry mouth (rare)
  • Nervousness or restlessness (rare, mainly in children)
  • Drowsiness (very rare, <8% of users)

Can I Take Both at Once?

No. Taking cetirizine and loratadine together is not recommended. Combining two antihistamines does not provide additional allergy relief — they compete for the same H1 receptors — and can increase side effects like drowsiness and dry mouth. Stick to one antihistamine at a time. If one antihistamine stops working for you (tachyphylaxis), switching to the other can sometimes restore effectiveness.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is cetirizine stronger than loratadine?
Yes, cetirizine has a higher affinity for H1 histamine receptors and is generally considered more potent. Studies consistently show it provides better relief for hives and intense allergy symptoms. However, it also causes more drowsiness than loratadine.
Does loratadine make you drowsy?
Loratadine is classified as a non-drowsy antihistamine. Less than 8% of users experience any sedation, compared to 10–15% with cetirizine. It is the preferred option for people who drive or need to stay alert.
Can I take cetirizine and loratadine on the same day?
No — do not take two antihistamines simultaneously. They target the same receptors and combining them adds no benefit while increasing side effect risk. If you want to switch from one to the other, wait 24 hours.
Which antihistamine is best for hives?
Cetirizine is the preferred OTC antihistamine for hives (urticaria). It provides faster and more complete suppression of the histamine-driven skin response compared to loratadine.
Is it safe to take antihistamines every day?
Yes — cetirizine and loratadine are safe for daily long-term use when taken at recommended doses. Many people take them throughout allergy season or year-round for perennial allergies. Consult your doctor for use beyond 3 months.

Conclusion

Both cetirizine and loratadine are excellent, safe antihistamines for allergy relief. The choice comes down to your symptom severity and daily lifestyle. Cetirizine wins for potency and hive control; loratadine wins for staying alert and non-drowsy daily use.
