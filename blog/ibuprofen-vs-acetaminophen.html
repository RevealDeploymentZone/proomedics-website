Ibuprofen vs Acetaminophen: Which Pain Reliever Should You Choose?

By Promedic Medical Team  |  August 6, 2026  |  9 min read

Ibuprofen and acetaminophen are two of the most widely used over-the-counter pain relievers in the US, yet many people are unsure which one to reach for. While both effectively relieve pain and reduce fever, they work through entirely different mechanisms — making each better suited for specific situations. This guide breaks down everything you need to know to choose the right one for your symptoms.

Key Takeaway: Ibuprofen is an anti-inflammatory, making it better for muscle pain, joint pain, and dental pain. Acetaminophen is gentler on the stomach and better for general pain or fever. Both are safe when used correctly — the differences matter most when you have specific health conditions or risk factors.

What Is Ibuprofen?

Ibuprofen 200mg (brand names: Advil, Motrin) is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID). It works by blocking enzymes called COX-1 and COX-2, which produce prostaglandins — the chemicals responsible for pain, inflammation, and fever. Because it directly targets inflammation, ibuprofen provides superior relief for conditions where swelling is the underlying cause of pain.

Ibuprofen is available OTC in 200mg tablets. Prescription-strength doses go up to 800mg. According to the National Library of Medicine, ibuprofen is one of the most commonly prescribed drugs worldwide and has a well-established safety profile when used as directed.

What Ibuprofen Treats Best

  • Muscle pain and soreness
  • Joint pain and arthritis
  • Dental pain and toothache
  • Menstrual cramps (dysmenorrhea)
  • Headaches and migraines
  • Back pain with inflammation
  • Fever
  • Post-surgical pain and swelling

What Is Acetaminophen?

Acetaminophen 500mg (brand name: Tylenol) works differently — it acts on the central nervous system to raise the pain threshold and lower fever, but it does NOT reduce inflammation. This means it is effective for pain and fever but will not address swelling or inflammatory conditions as effectively as ibuprofen.

Acetaminophen is one of the safest OTC medications when taken at recommended doses. The FDA recommends a maximum daily dose of 4,000mg (4g) for adults, though many experts suggest staying under 3,000mg daily to reduce liver risk, especially for people who drink alcohol.

What Acetaminophen Treats Best

  • General pain and headache
  • Fever in adults and children
  • Cold and flu symptoms
  • Pain for people with stomach ulcers or kidney disease
  • Pain management during pregnancy (consult your OB-GYN)
  • Post-surgical pain (as a supplement to stronger medications)

Which Works Better for Specific Pain Types?

Headache and Migraine

Both ibuprofen and acetaminophen are effective for headaches. However, research published in the Cochrane Library found that ibuprofen 400mg provided better headache relief than acetaminophen 1000mg. For migraines specifically, ibuprofen's anti-inflammatory action gives it an edge, though acetaminophen is a reasonable option when NSAIDs are contraindicated.

Dental Pain

Ibuprofen is the clear winner for dental pain. Tooth pain involves significant inflammation around the root, and ibuprofen's COX-inhibiting action directly targets this. Studies consistently show ibuprofen 400mg outperforms acetaminophen for dental pain by a meaningful margin. Some dentists recommend alternating ibuprofen and acetaminophen every 3 hours for post-extraction pain management.

Back Pain

For acute lower back pain, ibuprofen performs better when inflammation is involved (e.g., muscle strain, disc irritation). Acetaminophen has limited evidence for back pain specifically. If your back pain is from inflammation or injury, ibuprofen or naproxen 220mg (another NSAID with a longer duration) may provide more relief.

Menstrual Cramps

Ibuprofen is significantly more effective for menstrual pain. Menstrual cramps are caused by prostaglandins contracting the uterus — and since ibuprofen blocks prostaglandin production, it directly addresses the root cause. Acetaminophen can reduce the pain sensation but does not stop the cramping mechanism.

Fever

Both medications reduce fever effectively. Acetaminophen is often preferred for children because it has fewer contraindications in young patients. For adults, both work comparably, with ibuprofen sometimes providing longer-lasting fever reduction.

Arthritis and Joint Pain

Ibuprofen and other NSAIDs are first-line treatments for osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis. Their anti-inflammatory action reduces joint swelling and stiffness in ways acetaminophen cannot match. However, long-term NSAID use carries cardiovascular and kidney risks, so people with chronic joint pain should discuss options with their doctor.

Side Effects: Which Is Safer?

Ibuprofen Side Effects

  • Stomach irritation — the most common side effect; always take with food or milk
  • Stomach ulcers — risk increases with long-term use or high doses
  • Kidney stress — especially in dehydrated patients or those with pre-existing kidney disease
  • Blood pressure increase — can occur with regular use
  • Cardiovascular risk — slight increased risk of heart attack/stroke with high doses or prolonged use
  • Mild blood thinning — relevant if you're on anticoagulants

Taking omeprazole 20mg (a proton pump inhibitor) alongside ibuprofen can significantly reduce stomach irritation and ulcer risk. Read our full guide: Omeprazole for acid reflux.

Acetaminophen Side Effects

  • Liver damage — the main serious risk, occurring with overdose (over 4g/day) or with alcohol use
  • Skin reactions — rare but serious allergic reactions possible
  • Interactions with blood thinners — high doses can enhance warfarin's effect

Acetaminophen overdose causes more than 50,000 ER visits annually in the US according to the CDC, making it critical to track your total daily intake — especially since acetaminophen is present in many combination cold/flu products like NyQuil.

Warning: Never exceed the recommended daily dose of either medication. Do NOT combine acetaminophen with alcohol — this dramatically increases liver damage risk. Always check combination cold/flu products for hidden acetaminophen content to avoid accidental overdose.

Can You Take Ibuprofen and Acetaminophen Together?

Yes — ibuprofen and acetaminophen can be safely taken together because they work through different pathways and are processed by different organs (ibuprofen by the kidneys, acetaminophen by the liver). Some pain management protocols even recommend alternating them every 3–4 hours for stronger continuous pain relief without exceeding the maximum dose of either drug.

A 2019 study in The BMJ found that combining ibuprofen and acetaminophen was as effective as opioid medications for some types of acute pain. However, always consult your pharmacist or doctor before combining any medications, especially if you have underlying health conditions.

Who Should Avoid Ibuprofen?

  • People with stomach ulcers or gastrointestinal bleeding history
  • Those with chronic kidney disease
  • People with heart disease or on blood pressure medications
  • Those taking blood thinners (warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel)
  • Pregnant women in their third trimester
  • People taking other NSAIDs (like naproxen or aspirin) — never double up on NSAIDs

Who Should Avoid Acetaminophen?

  • People with liver disease or cirrhosis
  • Heavy alcohol drinkers (3+ drinks per day)
  • Those taking warfarin at high acetaminophen doses
  • Anyone already taking combination products containing acetaminophen (cold medicines, sleep aids)

Dosage Guide

Ibuprofen Dosage

  • Adults: 200–400mg every 4–6 hours as needed
  • Maximum OTC daily dose: 1,200mg (do not exceed without medical guidance)
  • Always take with food to reduce stomach irritation
  • Do not use for more than 10 days for pain without consulting a doctor

Acetaminophen Dosage

  • Adults: 325–1,000mg every 4–6 hours as needed
  • Maximum daily dose: 4,000mg (experts recommend staying under 3,000mg)
  • Can be taken on an empty stomach
  • Do not use for more than 10 days for pain without consulting a doctor

Ibuprofen vs Naproxen vs Aspirin: How Do Other OTC Pain Relievers Compare?

Beyond ibuprofen and acetaminophen, there are two other common OTC pain relievers worth knowing about:

  • Naproxen 220mg (Aleve) — also an NSAID like ibuprofen, but with a longer duration (8–12 hours vs. 4–6 hours). Better for sustained pain relief; not recommended for those with cardiovascular risks.
  • Aspirin 325mg — another NSAID with additional blood-thinning properties. Commonly used for cardiovascular protection. Less preferred for routine pain relief due to higher GI risk. Do not give to children (Reye's syndrome risk).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ibuprofen or acetaminophen better for pain?
Ibuprofen is generally better for inflammatory pain — dental pain, menstrual cramps, muscle soreness, and joint pain — because it reduces swelling at the source. Acetaminophen is better for general pain and fever with fewer stomach side effects. For headaches, both are similarly effective.
Can you take ibuprofen and acetaminophen together?
Yes. They work through different mechanisms and are safe to combine. Some pain management protocols alternate them every 3–4 hours for stronger continuous relief. Always stick to the recommended doses for each and consult your pharmacist if unsure.
Which is safer for the stomach?
Acetaminophen is gentler on the stomach. Ibuprofen can cause stomach irritation, ulcers, or bleeding, especially on an empty stomach or with long-term use. If you take ibuprofen regularly, consider also taking omeprazole 20mg for stomach protection.
Which is better for fever?
Both reduce fever effectively. Acetaminophen is often preferred for children. Ibuprofen may provide slightly longer-lasting fever reduction in adults. For high fevers that don't respond to one, doctors sometimes recommend alternating both.
Is it safe to take ibuprofen every day?
Daily ibuprofen use is not recommended without medical supervision. Long-term daily use increases the risk of stomach ulcers, kidney problems, and cardiovascular events. If you need daily pain relief, discuss safer long-term strategies with your doctor.

Conclusion

Both ibuprofen and acetaminophen are safe and effective when used correctly — the best choice depends on your specific pain type and health history. Choose ibuprofen for inflammatory conditions like dental pain, muscle soreness, joint pain, or menstrual cramps. Choose acetaminophen when you need to be gentle on your stomach, have kidney concerns, or are managing a fever with mild pain.
