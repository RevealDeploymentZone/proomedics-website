Vardenafil (Levitra) Complete Guide: Dosage, How It Works & Side Effects 2026

By Promedic Medical Team  |  July 25, 2026  |  10 min read

Vardenafil — sold under the brand name Levitra — is one of the most effective and well-tolerated erectile dysfunction medications available. Despite being overshadowed in name recognition by Viagra and Cialis, vardenafil has distinct pharmacological advantages that make it the preferred choice for many men, particularly those with diabetes or those who have found sildenafil less effective. This complete guide covers everything you need to know about vardenafil in 2026.

Vardenafil (Levitra) — Quick Reference

  • Drug class: PDE5 inhibitor
  • Brand name: Levitra (also available as generic vardenafil)
  • FDA approval: August 2003
  • Available doses: 2.5mg, 5mg, 10mg, 20mg
  • Standard dose: 10mg (range: 5–20mg depending on response)
  • Onset time: 25–60 minutes
  • Duration: 4–8 hours (longer than sildenafil)
  • Food interaction: High-fat meals delay absorption by ~1 hour
  • Orodispersible (ODT) version: Staxyn — dissolves under the tongue, no water needed
Key Takeaway: Vardenafil is approximately 10 times more potent than sildenafil on a milligram-per-milligram basis. It has the same mechanism of action as Viagra but is more selective for PDE5, which is why it causes fewer visual side effects. It works particularly well for men with diabetes.

What Is Vardenafil and How Does It Work?

Vardenafil is a phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5) inhibitor — the same class of drug as sildenafil (Viagra), tadalafil (Cialis), and avanafil (Spedra). All PDE5 inhibitors work through the same fundamental mechanism:

  1. Sexual stimulation triggers the release of nitric oxide from nerve endings in the penis
  2. Nitric oxide activates an enzyme that produces cyclic GMP (cGMP)
  3. cGMP relaxes the smooth muscle cells in penile blood vessels, allowing blood to flow in
  4. The enzyme PDE5 normally breaks down cGMP, ending the erection
  5. Vardenafil blocks PDE5, allowing cGMP to accumulate and sustain the erection

What distinguishes vardenafil from other PDE5 inhibitors is its greater selectivity for penile PDE5 over PDE6 (found in the retina) and PDE11 (found in muscles and testes). This explains why vardenafil causes fewer visual disturbances than sildenafil and fewer muscle aches than tadalafil — making it particularly well-tolerated in many men.

According to WebMD, vardenafil has been shown in clinical trials to be effective in approximately 65–80% of men with ED, including those with diabetes and other conditions that can make ED harder to treat.

How to Take Vardenafil Correctly

For best results from vardenafil:

  • Take 25–60 minutes before sexual activity — it typically starts working in about 30 minutes
  • Avoid high-fat meals before taking vardenafil — fatty foods significantly delay absorption and reduce peak blood levels
  • A light meal is fine and does not notably affect absorption
  • Limit alcohol — moderate drinking is acceptable, but excessive alcohol impairs erectile function independently
  • Sexual stimulation is required — vardenafil does not cause automatic erections; you must be aroused
  • Take no more than one dose per 24 hours
  • If it does not work the first time, try at least 6–8 times before concluding it is not effective for you

Vardenafil vs Sildenafil (Viagra): What's Different?

Both vardenafil and sildenafil are PDE5 inhibitors with similar mechanisms, but they have meaningful clinical differences:

Men who have tried sildenafil and experienced the blue-tinge visual side effect (cyanopsia), or found that a fatty meal ruined their timing, often find vardenafil a better experience. However, men with certain heart rhythm conditions (prolonged QT syndrome) should be cautious with vardenafil — discuss with a cardiologist first.

Vardenafil vs Tadalafil (Cialis): Which Is Better?

The most important difference between vardenafil and tadalafil is duration of action. Tadalafil lasts up to 36 hours; vardenafil lasts 4–8 hours. Men who want spontaneity and do not want to plan sex around a medication window tend to prefer tadalafil. Men who prefer a medication that clears their system more quickly — or who only want an effect for a defined time window — often prefer vardenafil. Vardenafil for Diabetic Men: Why It's a Top Choice

Men with type 2 diabetes are among those who have the most difficulty with ED medications — because diabetes damages both nerves and blood vessels, making it harder for any PDE5 inhibitor to achieve a full therapeutic effect. Clinical studies comparing PDE5 inhibitors specifically in diabetic men consistently show that vardenafil has among the highest response rates in this population.

A key study published in Diabetes Care found that vardenafil 10mg and 20mg produced significant improvements in erectile function in diabetic men, with over 72% of men reporting improved erections at the 20mg dose. This makes it a particularly important treatment option for the large number of diabetic men with ED. For more on diabetes and ED, read our guide on erectile dysfunction and diabetes.

Side Effects of Vardenafil

Like all PDE5 inhibitors, vardenafil can cause side effects — but these are typically mild and transient. The most commonly reported side effects according to the FDA and clinical trial data include:

Common Side Effects (affecting more than 5% of users)

  • Headache — most common side effect, typically mild; caused by vasodilation
  • Flushing — warmth or redness in the face and neck
  • Nasal congestion or runny nose
  • Indigestion or stomach discomfort
  • Dizziness — usually mild; more common when standing quickly

Less Common Side Effects

  • Back pain — less common than with tadalafil
  • Vision changes — blurred vision; much less common than with sildenafil due to higher PDE5 selectivity
  • Sensitivity to light (photophobia) — rare
  • Priapism (prolonged erection lasting more than 4 hours) — rare but requires immediate medical attention

Serious Side Effects: When to Seek Emergency Help

  • Sudden loss of vision in one or both eyes (NAION — non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy)
  • Sudden decrease or loss of hearing, with or without tinnitus
  • Chest pain or shortness of breath during sexual activity
  • Priapism (erection lasting more than 4 hours)
  • Severe dizziness or fainting

These serious events are rare but require immediate medical attention. Seek emergency care if they occur. For a broader overview of ED medication side effects, read our guide on ED medication side effects.

Important Drug Interactions

Vardenafil has several important drug interactions that can be dangerous:

  • Nitrates (GTN spray, isosorbide mononitrate/dinitrate) — absolutely contraindicated. Combined use causes a severe, potentially fatal drop in blood pressure. Never take vardenafil if you use nitrates for chest pain.
  • Alpha-blockers (tamsulosin, doxazosin for prostate or blood pressure) — can lower blood pressure significantly; start at 5mg vardenafil with caution
  • Antifungals (ketoconazole, itraconazole) and certain antibiotics (erythromycin, clarithromycin) — inhibit the liver enzyme that metabolises vardenafil, raising drug levels
  • Ritonavir and HIV protease inhibitors — significantly increase vardenafil blood levels; doses should not exceed 2.5mg every 72 hours
  • Antiarrhythmic drugs (amiodarone, sotalol, quinidine) — combined QT prolongation risk; avoid combination

Read our article on ED medications and blood pressure for a broader discussion of cardiovascular interactions.

Who Should Not Take Vardenafil?

Vardenafil is contraindicated in men who:

  • Take any form of nitrate medication (for chest pain or heart conditions)
  • Have severe cardiovascular disease or unstable angina
  • Have had a stroke or heart attack in the past 6 months
  • Have severe liver impairment (Child-Pugh Class C)
  • Have a personal or family history of prolonged QT syndrome (important distinction from sildenafil and tadalafil)
  • Have rare inherited retinal conditions (retinitis pigmentosa)

Vardenafil (Staxyn): The Dissolving Tablet Option

Vardenafil is also available in an orodispersible tablet (ODT) form called Staxyn in the US. Staxyn 10mg dissolves under the tongue and does not require water, making it more discreet and convenient for some men. Pharmacokinetically, Staxyn has slightly higher peak blood levels than the standard Levitra tablet at the same dose, so the two are not interchangeable on a dose-for-dose basis. Men switching between forms should consult a pharmacist.

Is Vardenafil Right for You?

Vardenafil may be the best choice if you:

  • Have tried sildenafil and experienced visual side effects (blue tinge, light sensitivity)
  • Have type 2 diabetes and found other ED medications less effective
  • Prefer a medication with a slightly longer duration than sildenafil but without the 36-hour window of tadalafil
  • Want a medication that is less affected by moderate food intake
  • Have experienced indigestion with sildenafil but want a similar-acting drug

Sildenafil or tadalafil may be preferable if you:

  • Want the lowest possible cost (sildenafil generics are widely available and very affordable)
  • Want the convenience of a 36-hour window (tadalafil)
  • Have a prolonged QT interval or take antiarrhythmic drugs (sildenafil is safer in this context)
  • Want a daily low-dose option (tadalafil 5mg daily)
